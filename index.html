Hi there!

I’m Anahita Bolourani (Ana), a Ph.D. candidate at UCLA who is passionate about artificial intelligence—especially diffusion models and large language models. My research blends advanced statistical theory with state‑of‑the‑art machine‑learning tools such as PyTorch and TensorFlow to push the limits of how we model, generate, and understand complex data. From image synthesis to seismic analysis and emotion‑aware systems, I explore ways generative models can drive real‑world impact.

News & Talks

COLM 2025 · Montreal

Social Impression Formation in LLMs

Conference on Language Modeling (COLM)

Thrilled to share that our paper on Social Impression Formation in LLMs is presented to #COLM2025. We ask: Do language models form social impressions like humans do? Using Affect Control Theory (ACT), we show LLMs tend to focus on the actor’s identity while largely ignoring behavior and recipient—and alignment/preference tuning doesn’t fix it (sometimes it amplifies it).

ACL 2025 · Poster

Poster: Mechanistic Emotion Inference in LLMs

Findings of ACL

If you’re into LLM interpretability, inference‑time steering, or cognitive modeling, please see our paper! We localize how autoregressive LLMs infer emotions, show functional regions that encode appraisal‑based representations, and causally steer generation by intervening on construed appraisal concepts— aligning outputs with theory and intuitive expectations.

Paper (arXiv)

Paper Spotlight — COLM 2025

Impact of LLM Alignment on Impression Formation in Social Interactions studies whether LLMs mirror Affect Control Theory in forming gendered impressions from Actor–Behavior–Recipient events. We find models are actor‑centric, with limited sensitivity to behavior/recipient, and that post‑training can shift or amplify these tendencies across model families and sizes.

  • Benchmark of ACT‑inspired scenarios to audit social impression formation.
  • Analyses across LLaMA‑3 derivatives (8B/70B) and GPT‑4.
  • Implications for alignment pipelines and safety auditing.
